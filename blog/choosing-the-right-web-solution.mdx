Different businesses need different digital tools — and choosing the wrong one can mean paying for features you'll never use, or launching with a platform that can't support the way you actually work. A freelance consultant has very different needs from an online retailer, and both have different needs from a business running complex internal workflows. Understanding which category your business falls into is the first — and most important — decision you'll make in your digital journey. Here's how to think it through.

## Compare Your Options

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Business Website">
    A professional business website is the right foundation for the vast majority of startups, service providers, and small businesses. It establishes your online presence, communicates your value proposition, and converts visitors into leads through clear calls to action and contact forms.

    **Best for:**
    - Service-based businesses (consultants, agencies, trades)
    - Professionals and sole practitioners
    - Startups building their brand and generating early enquiries
    - Personal brands and creatives showcasing a portfolio

    **Choose a business website when:**
    - You want to be found on Google and build organic visibility
    - Your primary goal is generating enquiries and leads
    - You need to showcase your services, portfolio, or team
    - You want a credible, professional presence that supports sales conversations

    **Pricing:** Starting from **R4,000** — a well-built, mobile-responsive, SEO-ready website that includes everything your business needs from day one.
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="E-commerce Store">
    An e-commerce store enables you to sell products directly through your website — managing inventory, processing payments, and fulfilling orders through a single integrated platform. It's a significant step up in complexity from a standard website, and the right choice when transactions are central to your business model.

    **Best for:**
    - Retailers selling physical products online
    - Artisans, makers, and creatives selling their own products
    - Businesses selling digital products, downloads, or subscriptions
    - Brick-and-mortar stores expanding into online sales

    **Choose an e-commerce store when:**
    - Customers need to browse, select, and purchase products directly through your site
    - You need inventory management, order tracking, and payment processing
    - You want to reach customers beyond your local area or physical storefront
    - You're ready to invest in a platform built to scale with your product catalogue

    **Pricing:** Quote-based — every e-commerce build is scoped according to the number of products, payment integrations, and custom functionality required. Request a quote to get an accurate estimate for your specific needs.
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Custom Web Application">
    A custom web application goes beyond a standard website to deliver purpose-built functionality — automating processes, managing data, enabling user accounts, or supporting workflows that no off-the-shelf platform can handle. It's the right choice when your business has genuinely unique operational requirements.

    **Best for:**
    - Businesses that need client or member portals with login functionality
    - Organisations requiring custom booking, scheduling, or workflow tools
    - Startups building a software product or platform as their core offering
    - Businesses that have outgrown the limitations of standard website platforms

    **Choose a custom application when:**
    - Your needs go meaningfully beyond what a standard website or e-commerce platform can provide
    - You need to automate internal processes or integrate with external systems
    - Your business model depends on users having accounts, dashboards, or personalised experiences
    - You're building a digital product that is itself the business

    **Pricing:** Quote-based — custom applications are scoped individually based on functionality, integrations, and complexity. The discovery process is where requirements are defined and a detailed quote is produced.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## How to Make Your Decision

Work through these four steps before committing to a direction. Taking the time to think clearly about your goals now will save you time, money, and frustration later.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify Your Primary Goal">
    Are you trying to generate leads and enquiries (business website), sell products directly (e-commerce), or automate and manage complex operations (custom application)? Your primary business goal is the single most important factor in determining the right solution. Be honest about what you actually need right now, not what you might eventually want.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Consider Your Audience">
    Think about who will be using your website or application. Are you selling to individual consumers (B2C) who need a seamless browsing and checkout experience? Pitching to other businesses (B2B) who need to evaluate your services and contact you? Or building a tool for your internal team? The answer shapes everything from your navigation structure to your feature requirements.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Plan for Growth">
    Consider where your business will be in 12 to 24 months. Will you need user accounts or a customer portal? Will your product catalogue grow significantly? Will you need to integrate with an accounting system, CRM, or logistics platform? Building with your growth trajectory in mind avoids the costly exercise of rebuilding from scratch when your needs evolve.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Request a Discovery Consultation">
    Once you have a sense of your goals, audience, and growth plans, the best next step is a conversation. A short discovery consultation allows iSibusiso to ask the right questions, understand your specific context, and confirm which solution — or combination of solutions — is the right fit for your business. This is where the right recommendation becomes clear.
  </Step>
</Steps>
