Not all websites are created equal. A website that looks good but loads slowly, breaks on mobile, or buries your contact details will cost you customers — quietly and consistently. The difference between a website that generates enquiries and one that doesn't often comes down to a handful of well-implemented features. Whether you're building your first site or evaluating an existing one, these ten essentials are non-negotiable for any small business serious about growing online.

## The 10 Website Essentials

<Steps>
  <Step title="Clear Value Proposition on the Homepage">
    Within three seconds of landing on your homepage, a visitor should know exactly what you do, who you do it for, and why they should choose you. Your headline and opening copy need to answer these questions before anything else. Vague taglines like "We deliver excellence" tell your visitor nothing — be specific, be direct, and make your value immediately obvious.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Mobile-Responsive Design">
    More than half of all web traffic in South Africa comes from mobile devices. If your website doesn't adapt gracefully to a smartphone screen — with readable text, tappable buttons, and no awkward horizontal scrolling — you're frustrating the majority of your visitors before they've read a single word. Mobile-responsiveness is not optional; it's foundational.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Fast Loading Speed">
    Every extra second your site takes to load increases the chance that a visitor will leave before it finishes. Studies show that pages taking longer than three seconds to load lose a significant portion of visitors. Fast hosting, optimised images, and clean code all contribute to speed — and Google rewards faster sites with better search rankings.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Contact Form or Booking System">
    Make it as easy as possible for a potential customer to reach you. A simple contact form — asking for a name, email address, and message — removes the friction of having to compose an email from scratch. If your business takes appointments, an integrated booking system goes even further by converting interest into a confirmed engagement instantly.
  </Step>

  <Step title="About Page With Your Story">
    People do business with people they trust. Your About page is your opportunity to introduce yourself, share why you started your business, and communicate the values that drive your work. For small businesses especially, the human story behind the brand is a genuine competitive advantage — don't hide it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Services or Product Catalogue">
    Your visitors need to understand exactly what you offer. A clearly structured services page or product catalogue — with descriptions, pricing where appropriate, and calls to action — guides visitors from interest to enquiry. Don't make potential customers guess at what you sell or how to get it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Social Proof (Testimonials and Case Studies)">
    Nothing builds confidence like evidence that others have worked with you and been satisfied. Include client testimonials, before-and-after case studies, or project showcases wherever relevant. Even two or three genuine, specific testimonials can meaningfully increase the likelihood of a new visitor reaching out.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Basic SEO (Meta Titles, Descriptions, Structured Data)">
    On-page SEO is the foundation of your discoverability on Google. Every page should have a unique, descriptive title tag and meta description. Proper heading structure (H1, H2, H3) helps both search engines and readers navigate your content. Structured data markup helps Google understand what your business is and where it's located — essential for local search visibility.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Google Analytics Setup">
    You can't improve what you don't measure. Google Analytics gives you visibility into how many people visit your site, which pages they view, how long they stay, and where they came from. This data is invaluable for making informed decisions about your content, your marketing, and your overall digital strategy. Set it up from day one.
  </Step>

  <Step title="SSL Certificate (HTTPS)">
    An SSL certificate encrypts the connection between your website and your visitors' browsers, protecting any data exchanged — including contact form submissions. It's visible as the padlock icon in your browser's address bar. Beyond security, Google flags sites without HTTPS as "Not Secure," which damages visitor trust and hurts your search rankings. Every website should be served over HTTPS, without exception.
  </Step>
</Steps>
